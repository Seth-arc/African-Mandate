# African Mandate: Sahel Arena — Game Trailer Script
**Format:** AI-generated images and video clips, fully narrated
**Target Runtime:** 1 minute 45 seconds
**Tone:** Grave, cinematic, documentary-weight — not action-game energy
**Music:** Low, sustained strings. Sparse percussion. Silence used deliberately.

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- All scene descriptions are written for AI image/video generation prompts
- Narration is designed for a single, calm, authoritative male voice — measured pace, no dramatic inflection peaks
- Text cards (on-screen supers) are marked separately from narration
- Total spoken word count: ~220 words at a deliberate pace (~130 wpm)

---

## SCRIPT

---

**[0:00 — 0:08]**

*SCENE: Aerial wide shot. An endless expanse of Sahel scrubland at golden hour. Dust haze on the horizon. No roads visible. No movement. Just scale.*

*Music enters — a single cello note, barely audible.*

**SUPER (slow fade in):**
> Africa. 2025.

---

**[0:08 — 0:22]**

*SCENE: A series of slow dissolves — cracked earth, a satellite map with red zones spreading, a line of displaced people walking across a ridge silhouetted against a pale sky, armed men in a pickup truck moving through scrub. No faces held long enough to identify. Impressionistic.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> The ink on the map no longer reflects the ground.
> Insurgencies control the trade routes.
> Climate erodes the arable land.
> Three million square kilometres — and the state is retreating from all of it.

---

**[0:22 — 0:34]**

*SCENE: Interior. A conference room in Addis Ababa. African Union flags. A folder slides across a table toward camera. Hands pick it up. We do not see a face — only the hands, and the word MANDATE on the cover.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> You have been appointed African Union Special Envoy to the Sahel.
> Your mandate is stabilisation.

*Beat of silence.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> Your reality is something else entirely.

---

**[0:34 — 0:52]**

*SCENE: A rapid sequence of three vignettes, each held for 4–5 seconds:*

*— A junta general in military dress, arms crossed, standing before a crowd of soldiers. Stern. Unmoved.*

*SCENE CUT:*

*— A civil society leader — a woman in traditional dress — speaking urgently to a small gathering in a village courtyard. Children visible in the background.*

*SCENE CUT:*

*— A drone strike aftermath. Smoke rising from a compound in a desert town. A news crew at a distance. A civilian figure in the foreground, motionless.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> Transitional governments demand legitimacy.
> Non-state actors control the resources.
> And every decision you make — every one — will be felt by eleven million people who had no say in your appointment.

---

**[0:52 — 1:08]**

*SCENE: The game interface appears — but rendered cinematically. Not a raw screenshot. A stylised overhead map of the Sahel, zones lighting up in amber and red. Resource bars slowly depleting. A dialogue card opens — a junta leader. Two options visible. Neither is comfortable.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> You have twenty turns. Forty-eight months.
> Three actions per turn.
> And never enough of anything — budget, personnel, political capital, time — to do what needs to be done.

*Music adds a low percussion pulse.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> You must choose which crises to mitigate.
> Which factions to leverage.
> Which compromises to swallow.

---

**[1:08 — 1:22]**

*SCENE: A montage — fast but not chaotic. Each image held for 1–2 seconds:*

*— Soldiers on patrol at dusk, long shadows.*
*— A handshake between two men in suits. Flags behind them.*
*— A dusty IDP camp. Aid workers moving between tents.*
*— An intelligence report on a desk, stamped UNVERIFIED.*
*— A phone call. A man listening. His expression does not change.*
*— A crowd. Uncertain. Watching.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> Security. Diplomacy. Governance. Humanitarian protection.
> None of these problems will wait for you to solve the others.

---

**[1:22 — 1:36]**

*SCENE: Slow zoom into the face of the civil society leader from earlier. She is waiting. Expecting something. The camera holds.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> Kinetic operations can secure territory.
> They cannot govern it.
> You cannot airstrike a drought.

*Silence. Three full seconds.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> Stability will require unpalatable compromises.

---

**[1:36 — 1:45]**

*SCENE: Black. Slow fade in on the hero image — the Sahel diplomats photograph, full frame, still. The title card appears.*

**SUPER (gold text, Playfair Display serif, centre frame):**
> African Mandate
> ——
> Sahel Arena

*Music resolves — not triumphantly. Just stops.*

**NARRATOR (V.O.):**
> A signature in Addis Ababa cannot stop the dust.

*SUPER fades to black.*

## DIRECTOR'S NOTES FOR AI GENERATION

**Visual palette:** Desaturated earth tones throughout. Ochre, sand, deep brown, muted olive. Avoid saturated colours except amber/gold for map UI elements.

**Lighting:** Harsh midday or golden hour for exterior shots. Fluorescent and shadowed for interiors. No soft, warm studio look.

**People:** African faces, African settings throughout. Military dress — West African styles. Civilian dress — regional traditional and contemporary mix. No Western military or iconography.

**Map UI shots:** Leaflet-style dark basemap with amber territory outlines. Red pulse on conflict zones. Minimal, clinical, serious.

**Avoid:** Combat action sequences, explosions as spectacle, heroic framing of the player character, any imagery that aestheticises conflict. The tone is weight, not excitement.
