## 1. Executive Overview

### 1.1 Game Identity

**Title:** African Mandate: Sahel Arena  
**Genre:** Strategic Crisis Simulation / Political Drama  
**Core Themes:**
- African-led solutions to African challenges
- Systems thinking in complex humanitarian crises
- Ethical leadership under pressure
- Cultural sensitivity and local ownership
- Long-term stability over short-term victories

**Narrative Pillars:**
1. **Authenticity:** Grounded in real regional dynamics, cultural contexts, and historical patterns
2. **Agency:** Player choices matter and create meaningful divergent outcomes
3. **Complexity:** No simple solutions; every decision has trade-offs
4. **Hope:** Despite challenges, positive change is possible through thoughtful action
5. **Respect:** All actors, communities, and cultures treated with dignity

### 1.2 Story Premise

The Sahel region stands at a precipice. A convergence of crises—jihadist insurgency, military coups, climate-induced resource scarcity, youth unemployment, and great power competition—threatens to destabilize the entire region and beyond. Traditional interventions have failed. External military solutions have created dependency and resentment. The African Union and ECOWAS recognize that only African-led, comprehensive approaches can succeed.

You are appointed as the first-ever **Special Envoy for Sahel Stabilization**, a newly created position that reports directly to the AU Peace and Security Council and coordinates with ECOWAS. Your mandate is unprecedented: unite fractured coalitions, restore constitutional order, address root causes of instability, and prove that African institutions can resolve the continent's most complex crises.

**Core Dramatic Question:** Can you navigate competing interests, limited resources, and mounting pressure to bring stability to the Sahel, or will the region descend into further chaos?

### 1.3 Target Experience

**Emotional Journey:**
- **Opening:** Overwhelming urgency, weight of responsibility, slight intimidation
- **Early Game:** Growing confidence, learning systems, making impactful decisions
- **Mid Game:** Moral complexity, difficult trade-offs, seeing consequences unfold
- **Late Game:** Strategic mastery, long-term planning, witnessing cumulative impact
- **Ending:** Reflection on choices, satisfaction (or regret), desire to replay

**Player Transformation:**
- From reactive crisis manager → Proactive strategic architect
- From seeing isolated problems → Understanding interconnected systems
- From seeking quick wins → Building sustainable solutions
- From external observer → Invested stakeholder in African futures

## 3. World Building & Context

### 3.1 The Sahel Region - Overview

**Geographic Scope:**
- Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad (core focus)
- Broader regional context: Mauritania, Nigeria, Benin, Togo
- Strategic corridors and border zones

**Population & Demographics:**
- Total population: ~80 million across core countries
- Youth bulge: 60%+ under 25 years old
- Ethnic diversity: Tuareg, Fulani, Hausa, Mossi, Songhai, Arab, dozens more
- Religious landscape: Predominantly Muslim with animist traditions, Christian minorities
- Urbanization: Rapid, unplanned growth in capitals; rural areas remain majority

**Economic Realities:**
- GDP per capita: Among world's lowest ($500-$1,500)
- Primary sectors: Subsistence agriculture, pastoralism, small-scale mining
- Formal employment: Less than 10% of workforce
- Informal economy: Dominant, includes smuggling and gray markets
- Natural resources: Gold, uranium, oil (often poorly managed, fueling corruption)

**Climate Crisis:**
- Temperature rise: 1.5x global average
- Sahara expansion: Southward desertification
- Lake Chad: Shrunk 90% since 1960s
- Rainfall: Increasingly erratic, devastating droughts and flash floods
- Resource competition: Water, pasture land, agricultural zones

### 3.2 Historical Context (Pre-Game)

**Colonial Legacy (1900-1960):**
- French colonial administration created artificial borders
- Resource extraction economy established
- Traditional governance systems disrupted
- Education system favored urban elites
- Military structures built for external control, not internal development

**Independence Era (1960-1990):**
- Single-party states and military dictatorships
- Cold War proxy conflicts
- Brief democratic experiments
- Structural adjustment programs devastate social services
- Growing resentment toward postcolonial governments

**First Tuareg Rebellions (1990s-2000s):**
- Tuareg communities marginalized post-independence
- Rebellions in Mali and Niger for autonomy
- Peace agreements signed but poorly implemented
- Seeds of current crisis planted

**Libya Collapse & Regional Spillover (2011):**
- Gaddafi's fall releases fighters and weapons into Sahel
- Tuareg fighters return from Libyan military
- Arms proliferation destabilizes region
- Creates power vacuum that jihadist groups exploit

**Malian Crisis (2012-2013):**
- Tuareg rebellion joins with jihadist groups (AQIM)
- Northern Mali briefly falls to insurgent coalition
- Tuareg-jihadist alliance fractures
- French Operation Serval intervenes
- Malian state never fully recovers sovereignty

**Regional Escalation (2014-2023):**
- JNIM (Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin) consolidates power
- Islamic State Greater Sahara (ISGS) emerges
- Insurgency spreads to Burkina Faso, Niger
- Local ethnic militias form in response
- Violence escalates dramatically

**Coup Cascade (2020-2023):**
- Military coups in Mali (2020, 2021), Guinea (2021), Burkina Faso (2022), Niger (2023)
- Juntas form Alliance of Sahel States (AES), reject ECOWAS
- Russian Wagner Group enters Mali, Burkina Faso
- French forces expelled from multiple countries
- Regional institutions' credibility challenged

**Crisis Point (Game Start - 2024):**
- Peak violence: Attacks occurring weekly across region
- Humanitarian emergency: 6+ million displaced
- Food insecurity: 30+ million at risk
- Democratic backsliding: All core states under military rule
- External interference: Russia, France, US, China competing for influence
- International isolation: ECOWAS sanctions on junta states

### 3.3 Cultural & Social Landscape

**Ethnic Complexity:**
- No ethnic majority in most countries
- Historical economic niches: Tuareg (traders, pastoralists), Fulani (herders), Hausa (merchants), Songhai (farmers)
- Inter-ethnic relations range from cooperation to conflict
- Colonial favoritism created lasting resentments
- Resource competition exacerbates tensions

**Religious Dynamics:**
- Sufi Islam: Traditional, dominant, emphasizes mysticism and tolerance
- Salafi/Wahhabi Islam: Growing, especially among youth, more puritanical
- Animist traditions: Still practiced, often syncretized with Islam
- Inter-religious harmony: Historically strong but under pressure

**Traditional Authority:**
- Chiefs, sultans, traditional councils retain significant legitimacy
- Often more trusted than government officials
- Play crucial role in mediation and conflict resolution
- Marginalized by colonial and postcolonial governments
- Revival of interest in traditional governance

**Youth Dynamics:**
- Median age: 16-17 years
- School enrollment: 40-60% (lower for girls, rural areas)
- Unemployment: 70%+ for youth
- Urban drift: Rural youth migrating to cities
- Grievances: Exclusion from political process, lack of opportunities, corruption
- Recruitment: Vulnerable to both jihadist groups and government militias

**Gender Issues:**
- Women's literacy: 20-30% in many areas
- Political representation: Minimal
- Early marriage: Common
- Economic roles: Critical in agriculture, markets, but often unrecognized
- Conflict impact: Displacement, sexual violence, loss of livelihoods
- Emerging activism: Women's peace coalitions gaining voice

### 3.4 The Crisis - Current State (Game Opening)

**Security Situation:**
- **Intensity:** 4,000+ conflict deaths per year (2023)
- **Geographic Spread:** Violence in 60%+ of Sahel territory
- **Attack Types:** IED ambushes, village raids, assassinations, kidnappings
- **Actors:** JNIM, ISGS, ethnic militias, government forces, Wagner, local defense groups
- **Civilian Toll:** Highest casualties from all sides; protection of populations failing

**Humanitarian Crisis:**
- **Displaced:** 6+ million IDPs and refugees
- **Food Insecurity:** 30+ million at risk of severe hunger
- **Health:** Collapse of services in conflict zones; disease outbreaks
- **Education:** 6,000+ schools closed due to insecurity
- **Access:** Humanitarian workers increasingly targeted; large areas unreachable

**Political Vacuum:**
- **Coups:** Military juntas in Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad
- **Legitimacy Crisis:** Constitutional orders suspended; promised transitions delayed
- **Regional Isolation:** AES states withdraw from ECOWAS cooperation
- **Governance Failure:** State presence absent from large swathes of territory
- **Corruption:** Rampant; fuels both grievances and insurgent financing

**Economic Collapse:**
- **Trade Disruption:** ECOWAS sanctions; insecurity blocking commerce
- **Currency Pressure:** CFA franc tensions with France
- **Investment Flight:** Foreign companies withdrawing
- **Informal Economy:** Growth of smuggling, trafficking, illegal mining
- **Youth Desperation:** Rising unemployment drives recruitment into armed groups

**Climate & Resource Stress:**
- **Drought:** Worst in 40 years in parts of region
- **Farmer-Herder Conflict:** Competition for diminishing resources
- **Urban Pressure:** Cities swelling with climate refugees
- **Food Production:** Declining yields; rising prices
- **Water Scarcity:** Conflicts over wells and water points

**External Interference:**
- **Russia/Wagner:** Military cooperation with Mali, Burkina Faso; disinformation campaigns
- **France:** Expelled from Mali, Niger; reduced presence; conflicted about role
- **United States:** Counter-terrorism focus; drone bases; limited effectiveness
- **China:** Economic investments; infrastructure; non-interference policy
- **Turkey, UAE, others:** Competing for influence through various means

**Regional Organizations:**
- **ECOWAS:** Sanctions on juntas; threatened military intervention; credibility damaged
- **African Union:** Suspended junta governments; seeking solutions; limited enforcement
- **G5 Sahel:** Joint force concept; underresourced, coordination challenges
- **AES (Alliance of Sahel States):** Junta solidarity; anti-intervention stance

## 5. Tutorial & Onboarding

### 5.1 Tutorial Structure

**Philosophy:** 
- Learn by doing, not by reading pages of text
- Context-sensitive help that appears when needed
- Minimizable but always accessible
- Builds confidence through small successes
- Respects player intelligence—doesn't over-explain

**Delivery Method:**
1. **Video Modal:** Opens automatically for first-time players
2. **Slide Carousel:** 8-10 core concept slides
3. **Minimized Capsule:** Persistent bottom-right corner element
4. **Contextual Tooltips:** Appear during first encounters with mechanics
5. **Practice Scenario:** Optional low-stakes scenario to test learning

### 5.2 Tutorial Content Outline

**SLIDE 1: WELCOME**

**Visual:** AU/ECOWAS emblems, map of Sahel highlighting player's area of operations

**Text:**
> "Welcome, Special Envoy. This briefing will prepare you for your critical mission to stabilize the Sahel. You can minimize this tutorial at any time and return by clicking the help icon."

**Audio:** Welcoming, professional tone

---

**SLIDE 2: YOUR ROLE & MANDATE**

**Visual:** Organizational chart showing player position, reporting lines to AU/ECOWAS

**Text:**
> "You are the Special Envoy for Sahel Stabilization—a unique position coordinating African Union, ECOWAS, and partner efforts. Your mandate: protect civilians, restore stability, address root causes of conflict. You have unprecedented authority, but limited resources. Use them wisely."

**Key Concepts:**
- You are African-led initiative (this matters to legitimacy)
- You coordinate, not command (must build coalitions)
- Success measured in long-term stability, not just security

---

**SLIDE 3: UNDERSTANDING THE SITUATION ROOM**

**Visual:** Annotated screenshot of main interface

**Text:**
> "Your Situation Room is mission control. Here you'll see:"

**Annotations point to:**
1. **Interactive Map:** Current crisis hotspots, your assets, actor positions
2. **Resource Panel:** Budget, personnel, political capital, intel points
3. **Metrics Dashboard:** Stability, Insurgency, Support, Legitimacy, Synergy

6. **Action Menu:** Available interventions and strategies

---

**SLIDE 4: KEY METRICS EXPLAINED**

**Visual:** Five core metrics with visual indicators

**1. Stability (Target: >55)**
- Measures security, governance functionality, public order
- Low stability = increased violence, humanitarian crisis
- Raised through: Security operations, governance support, economic development

**2. Insurgency Level (Target: <45)**
- Measures strength and reach of armed groups
- High insurgency = loss of territory, attacks on civilians
- Lowered through: Military action, counternarratives, addressing grievances

**3. Global Legitimacy (Target: >55)**
- Measures international and regional support for your mission
- Low legitimacy = reduced aid, sanctions, isolation
- Raised through: Diplomatic engagement, human rights protection, constitutional progress

**4. Civilian Support (Target: >50)**
- Measures local population's trust and cooperation
- Low support = insurgent recruitment, intelligence blackout
- Raised through: Service delivery, protection, inclusive governance

**5. Regional Synergy (Target: >0.55)**
- Measures cooperation between ECOWAS, AU, and national governments
- Low synergy = uncoordinated actions, contradictory policies
- Raised through: Joint operations, policy harmonization, confidence-building

---

**SLIDE 5: RESOURCES & ALLOCATION**

**Visual:** Resource panel breakdown with example costs

**Resources You Manage:**
- **Financial Budget:** For all operations and programs
- **External Aid:** Conditional funding from partners
- **Personnel:** Security forces, aid workers, technical experts
- **Political Capital:** Influence to negotiate, pressure actors
- **Intelligence:** Intel points to reveal threats, gather information
- **Time:** Each turn represents 3 months—urgency matters

**Critical Principle:**
> "Every resource spent has opportunity costs. Spending on security means less for development. Time spent negotiating delays emergency response. Choose wisely."

---

**SLIDE 6: ACTORS & RELATIONSHIPS**

**Visual:** Network diagram of major actors

**Text:**
> "You don't act alone. The Sahel is crowded with state and non-state actors, each with their own interests:"

**Categories:**
1. **State Actors:** Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, Chad governments (often military juntas)
2. **Non-State Armed Groups:** JNIM, ISGS, local militias
3. **Community Leaders:** Traditional chiefs, religious authorities, women's groups
4. **Regional Organizations:** ECOWAS, AU, G5 Sahel, AES
5. **External Powers:** France, Russia/Wagner, US, UN, China

**Key Insight:**
> "No actor is entirely friend or foe. Build coalitions tactically. Your relationship with each actor affects what options are available."

---

**SLIDE 7: DECISIONS & CONSEQUENCES**

**Visual:** Decision tree showing branching outcomes

**Text:**
> "Every choice creates ripples. Military action may reduce insurgency but harm civilian support if executed poorly. Negotiation may build legitimacy but be seen as weakness by hardliners. There are no perfect solutions—only trade-offs."

**Example Illustrated:**
- **Decision:** Send troops to protect a village
  - **Positive:** Insurgency reduced, some civilian support gained
  - **Negative:** Budget depleted, risk of civilian casualties, troops diverted from other areas
  - **Cascading Effect:** Other villages now expect protection; failure to deliver reduces legitimacy

**Lesson:**
> "Think systemically. Consider second and third-order effects. Build toward long-term stability, not just crisis response."

---

**SLIDE 8: ACTS & PROGRESSION**

**Visual:** Five-act structure with milestone markers

**Text:**
> "Your mission unfolds across five acts, each approximately 3-5 turns (9-15 months). Achievements in early acts shape options in later acts."

**Act Structure:**
1. **Act 1: Crisis Response** - Stabilize immediate threats, prove credibility
2. **Act 2: Coalition Building** - Unite actors, establish coordination
3. **Act 3: Root Causes** - Address governance, economics, climate issues
4. **Act 4: Consolidation** - Secure gains, expand stability zones
5. **Act 5: Transition** - Prepare for handover to local/regional leadership

**Progression Note:**
> "Your performance is measured at the end of Act 5. Multiple endings exist based on your metrics and key decisions."

---

**SLIDE 9: WIN CONDITIONS & GAME OVER**

**Visual:** Metrics chart showing success thresholds

**Success Criteria (All must be met):**
- Average Stability: >55
- Average Insurgency Level: <45
- Global Legitimacy: >55
- Regional Synergy: >0.55
- Civilian Support: >50

**Game Over Conditions:**
- Any single metric remains in critical range for 3 consecutive turns
- Major humanitarian disaster occurs due to player negligence
- Complete loss of international legitimacy (sanctions, mandate revocation)
- Player character death/removal from position (rare, specific choices)

**Encouragement:**
> "Failure is not the end—it's an opportunity to learn. Each playthrough teaches you more about these complex systems."

---

**SLIDE 10: FINAL GUIDANCE**

**Visual:** Collage of Sahelian faces—civilians, leaders, youth

**Text:**
> "Remember: You represent the hope that African institutions can solve African crises. The people of the Sahel are depending on you."

**Core Principles:**
1. **Protect Civilians First** - Military metrics matter less than human safety
2. **Think Long-Term** - Sustainable solutions over quick fixes
3. **Build Local Ownership** - Empower regional actors, don't create dependency
4. **Embrace Complexity** - No single action will solve everything
5. **Maintain Integrity** - Your reputation is your most valuable resource

**Final Line:**
> "The Situation Room is ready. Your mission begins now. Good luck, Special Envoy."

## DESIGN NOTES FOR IMPLEMENTATION

### Narrative Pacing
- Act 5 should feel accelerated—clock is ticking
- Every turn in Act 5 represents 3-4 weeks (not 3 months)
- More frequent decision points
- Higher stakes
- Less time for elaborate planning

### Emotional Tone
- Act 5 should evoke reflection, urgency, and closure
- Player should feel weight of time running out
- Endings should feel earned, not arbitrary
- Even "bad" endings should have dignity and honesty

### Player Agency Preservation
- Even in final turns, player choices matter
- Multiple viable paths to each ending tier
- No single decision determines ending (cumulative)
- Endings reflect player's overall strategic approach, not just final metrics

### Replayability Hooks
- Endings should inspire curiosity: "What if I had done X?"
- Unlockable content rewards multiple playthroughs
- Different approaches yield genuinely different experiences
- Easter eggs and hidden paths for dedicated players

### Educational Value
- Even in failure, player learns real lessons about crisis response
- Endings contextualize real-world intervention challenges
- Post-game content encourages deeper engagement with material
- Overall experience should be both entertaining AND educational
