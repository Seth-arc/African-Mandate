1. Business Developer Eligibility
Your game must be developed by a business of any size (private, publicly held, NGO, commercially funded, state‑run educational institution where the game is not a student product, or not‑for‑profit organization).

Indie developers are explicitly treated as business developers.
​
Government-developed or government‑funded games cannot use the Government developer category; they fall under Business if a business entity is involved.
​

2. Game Qualification Requirements
Game must have clearly defined, measurable learning objectives and a clearly identified challenge/problem.
Must provide positive/negative feedback related to the learning objectives.
Must be an original game, not a mod or novel use of an existing game.
Intended audience must be 12 years or older.
Game must be beta stage or better (feature complete) by the entry deadline, with at least one fully playable level.
​

If previously submitted to SGSCE, you must show significant enhancement and explain what changed.
​

3. Category & Audience Choices
At submission, you must select:

Developer category: Business.
​
Target audience award category:

Government Audience (if built specifically for government/defence), or
General/Business Audience (corporate training, general education, health, public interest, etc.).
​

4. Application Materials You Must Prepare
Complete the official Submission Form on the SGSCE website by the Game Submission Deadline.
​
Submit the game build using the platform‑specific instructions (PC, mobile, XR, web) in Appendix A.
​
If your game needs special hardware (beyond PC + monitor + keyboard + mouse), you must supply at least one unit for evaluation and more if you are a finalist.

Provide adequate English translation if the game is not in English (install, controls, objectives, outcomes).
​
For industry (business) entrants, pay the non‑refundable application fee of GBP 500 via the payment link on the website; fee is not waived for business entries.
​

Provide required promotional assets:

High‑resolution company logo (if business entry).
​

High‑resolution game logo.
​

High‑resolution gameplay screenshots (≥300 DPI, ~1000×1000 px, JPEG/PNG/GIF, only in‑game graphics).
​

Two gameplay videos: one ≤60 seconds for promo; one longer video for evaluators (no length limit).
​

5. Key Judging Criteria (Design Focus)
Learning: how well players achieve the learning objectives, how learning occurs through gameplay, and how performance is assessed.
​

Technical quality: ease of installation, stability, graphics, sound, and overall presentation.
​

Gameplay: usability, engagement/fun, and cohesion of the overall experience.
​

Innovation: novel techniques or features (hardware, instructional design, game design, content, etc.).
​

6. Conference & Presence Requirements
You must be eligible to attend DSET (in person or virtually); minimum age 12; under‑18s require a parent/guardian on the team and as chaperone.
​

If selected as a finalist, you must:

Attend the Conference (or send an approved proxy, 18+).
​

Demonstrate your game at the SGSCE booth; kiosk PC and basic peripherals are provided.
​

Be present (physically or virtually) to win an award (unless this rule is formally suspended).
​

You must cover your own travel, accommodation, meals, and extra equipment costs.
​

7. XR / Special Hardware Duties (If Applicable)
XR finalists must provide the headset hardware (SGSCE provides a PC, or you may bring your own).
​

You must follow defined health and sanitisation protocols between demos (clean lenses, headset surfaces, etc.), and accept any additional requirements notified before the conference.
​

Failure to comply with mandatory health/sanitation requirements can lead to withdrawal from the conference.
​

8. Legal, IP, and Compliance Essentials
You must have all necessary rights and licenses for engines, middleware, assets, likenesses, and third‑party elements so the Host can evaluate and publicly demonstrate the game.
​

Intellectual property rights remain with you, but entry materials are not returned (except special equipment if pre‑arranged and at your cost).
​

Do not submit classified, restricted, or export‑controlled content; ensure compliance with all export controls for worldwide evaluators.
​

You grant permission for use of your name, logo, game, videos, screenshots for Challenge and Conference promotion without additional compensation (where allowed by law).
​

You must ensure your game and conduct do not defame, violate privacy, or break applicable laws; offensive or inflammatory entries may be disqualified.
​

9. Disqualification Triggers to Avoid
Failing to meet any eligibility or submission requirements in the Official Rules.
​

Attempting to circumvent rules, disrupt the Challenge, or act unsportsmanlike.
​

Embedding tracking software to collect play metrics in your entry (likely blocked and prohibited).
​

Ignoring mandatory health/sanitation measures or other conference protocols.
​
